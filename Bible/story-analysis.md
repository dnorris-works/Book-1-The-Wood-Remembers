## What To Do With This Analysis

### Genre & Subgenre
Confirm this matches how you intend to position the book. If it surprises you, consider whether the manuscript is sending signals you didn't intend — or whether you've found a more marketable angle than you realized. Lock in the subgenre before you write your back-cover copy.

### Amazon Categories
Log into your KDP dashboard and verify these browse-node paths still exist — Amazon reorganizes categories periodically. You can select up to 3 categories at publish time (as of the mid-2023 KDP overhaul). The limit is firm; you can no longer request extras via KDP support. Use all three slots — one broader category for general visibility, and two specific niche categories where you have a realistic shot at a #1 bestseller tag. Use Publisher Rocket's Category Search to check each path for ghost categories and confirm the sales rank needed to hit #1 before committing. Then run **Author Tools → Rocket: Analyze Categories** to get a full data-driven recommendation.

### Comp Authors & Titles
Read the comps you haven't read yet — especially the most recent ones. Study their cover design, back-cover copy, price point, series naming conventions, and review language. Your comps are your packaging blueprint. Use them in your query letter or AMS ad copy as reader signposts: *"Fans of [Comp Author] will love…"*

### Core Emotional Themes
These are your marketing hooks. Each theme is a potential tagline, an AMS ad headline, or a newsletter subject line. Write 2–3 one-sentence pitches that lead with the dominant emotional promise rather than the plot.

### Tropes
Tropes are searchable. Readers use sites like The StoryGraph and Goodreads lists to find books by trope. Add the confirmed tropes to your book's Goodreads page and any reader-community posts. They also belong in your AMS keyword list alongside the seed keywords below.

### Target Reader Profile
This profile drives every platform decision. Use it to choose which Facebook or BookTok communities to engage with, what reader magnets to write, and how to write your author bio. If the profile surprises you, revisit your cover and back-cover copy — packaging must speak directly to this reader on first glance.
