## Full Issue Details

### Issue 1 — Act 1: Chapter 4 is a redundant holding chapter
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 4 — What the Touch Left

**Problem:** Chapter 4 has no event. It processes the Encounter (ch. 3) and watches the road change — the same ground the opening of ch. 5 covers. The "Changed Wood" section does valuable thematic work, but the "Thirtieth Year" section duplicates what ch. 5 opens with (urgency on the road, his step appearing, two kinds of visitors beginning to emerge).

**Fix:** Compress ch. 4 to its essential beat — the changed heartwood — and absorb the "Thirtieth Year" material into ch. 5's opening. This shortens Act 1 by roughly half a chapter without losing anything readers need.

### Issue 2 — Act 1: Chapters 5 and 6 structural overlap
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapters:** 5 — The Pressure Builds, 6 — Sacred Eight

**Problem:** The "Two Kinds of Visitors" section in ch. 6 (followers vs. official delegations) is setup material that should precede the martyrdoms, not follow them. Currently it appears after Miriam and Benjamin's deaths, which means the reader learns about the dual-stream dynamic after it has already produced its first casualties. This undercuts the dramatic irony the section should create.

**Fix:** Move "Two Kinds of Visitors" from ch. 6 to the close of ch. 5, replacing the current ending on Thomas bar-Judah. Let ch. 6 open directly with Miriam's return and the soldiers' arrival, with the reader already carrying the knowledge of the two streams converging. Thomas bar-Judah's death could either remain in ch. 5 as its climax (before the dual-stream section) or be absorbed into ch. 6's martyrdom sequence.

### Issue 3 — Act 1: Levi continuity error in chapter 8
**Priority:** High
**Chapter:** 8 — The Woodcutters

**Problem:** The chapter opens with Centurion Varro described as "the man who had tied Levi to my trunk and the man who had ordered the soldiers down the hill when there was nothing left to do." This is the first and only mention of Levi anywhere in the manuscript. There is no earlier scene in which Varro ties anyone to the tree, no character named Levi has appeared, and the reference has no antecedent the reader can find. This is either a continuity error from a cut scene or a draft remnant.

**Fix:** Either (a) remove the Levi reference entirely and describe Varro simply as a centurion the tree recognizes from the hillside's official visitors — "the man who had led the assessment delegation in the autumn, the one who had looked southeast toward the Temple for a long time without moving" — or (b) add a brief earlier reference to Levi's execution in chs. 5–6 to establish the antecedent. Option (a) is simpler and loses nothing.

### Issue 4 — Act 1: Generic section titles in chapters 16–18

They describe content rather than capturing the quality of the tree's experience. The prose in these chapters is specific and precise; the section titles are not.

**Fix:** Revise section titles to match the specificity of the prose. Examples: "The Weight of Breathing" → "Each Breath Bought" or simply remove section titles from these chapters and let the prose run continuously, since the chapters are short enough not to need navigation headers.

### Issue 6 — Act 2: Transition jolt inside chapter 24
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 24 — Scattered Consciousness

**Problem:** The chapter opens with the tree's abstract POV ("Two thousand years is not long, for wood" / "The Interval" section) and then pivots directly to Sarah in the Vatican Archive with no bridge. The whiplash isn't between chs. 23 and 24 — it's inside ch. 24 itself, where the reader is in dispersed tree consciousness and then suddenly in a 2025 reading room. The date stamp (*February 23, 2025 — Vatican Apostolic Archive*) helps but doesn't provide a narrative bridge.

**Fix:** Add a single transitional sentence at the close of the "Interval" section, before the Vatican Archive heading, in which the tree registers being attended to — something like: *"Then a woman put on white cotton gloves and sat down at a table in Rome."* This one sentence bridges the abstract centuries and the specific present without over-explaining the shift. (Note: this sentence already exists in the chapter's body — move it to serve as the bridge between the tree-voice section and Sarah's section.)

### Issue 7 — Act 2: Chapters 25 and 26 share nearly identical closing lines
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapters:** 25 — Empire's End, 26 — The New Work

**Problem:** Chapter 25's tree-voice close ends: *"The same hands. Different work. That is what it costs, sometimes, to become the thing you were broken into being."* Chapter 26's tree-voice close ends: *"The same hands. Different work."* Because ch. 26 follows immediately after ch. 25, the reader encounters the echo within pages. The callback is intentional but lands as repetition rather than resonance at this proximity.

**Fix:** Vary one of the two closes. The ch. 25 close is the stronger and more complete statement — keep it. Revise ch. 26's close to something that builds on it rather than echoing it. Ch. 26's close could focus on the quality of *sustained* protection vs. the single act of conversion: the difference between what breaks you into something new and what you do with the new shape across years.

### Issue 8 — Act 2: Chapter 27 four sections orbit the same beat
**Priority:** High
**Chapter:** 27 — Second Light (Discovery)

**Problem:** The discovery chapter has five section headers ("The Initial Find," "Professional Curiosity," "Personal Faith," "The Unusual Preservation," "The Journey Begins") but the sections don't escalate — they circle the same moment (Sarah finds the fragments, is astonished, has a faith conversation with Kowalski) four times. The preservation anomaly is introduced, then re-introduced, then re-introduced again. Kowalski's speech about Christians dying for these fragments appears in essentially the same form in three different sections.

**Fix:** Consolidate to two or three sections with clear escalation: (1) the find itself — purely physical, no interpretation; (2) the anomaly registered and the first real conversation about what it means; (3) the decision to go to Rome. Cut the repetitive pastoral dialogue in "Professional Curiosity" and "Personal Faith" — Kowalski can make his theological point once, clearly, rather than three times gently.

### Issue 9 — Act 2: Father backstory told twice
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapters:** 28 — Personal Histories, 29 — The Harvard Crisis

**Problem:** The emotional material about Sarah's father — his faith crisis during her mother's illness, his loss of certainty, his eventual arrival at a deeper kind of presence — is developed substantially in ch. 28 (the kitchen conversation with Kowalski, including the specific Sunday afternoon memory of refinishing the table). The tribunal in ch. 29 then invokes Sarah's father and his crisis again as context for her "religious bias." The double appearance dilutes the emotional weight of the material.

**Fix:** Ch. 28 should be the primary scene for the father material — it already has the right depth and the right frame (Kowalski drawing it out gently). In ch. 29, the tribunal's reference to her background should be limited to a single line that the reader recognizes without needing the backstory repeated. Something like "her Catholic undergraduate degree, her father the deacon" — a mention, not a development.

### Issue 10 — Act 2: Chapter 29 Holloway setup with no payoff
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 29 — The Harvard Crisis

**Problem:** The chapter opens with Sarah bringing coffee to Dr. Holloway before the tribunal, and Holloway behaving with portentous guilt — not meeting Sarah's eyes, a "particular stillness," choosing words from "behind glass." This is a clear setup for Holloway to have betrayed Sarah's confidence to the committee. But Holloway never appears again, and the betrayal (when it comes in ch. 35) is Webb's data theft, not anything Holloway did. The Holloway setup creates an expectation that is never resolved.

**Fix:** Either (a) cut the Holloway opening scene and start the chapter at "The email had been waiting since 6 AM" — the tribunal scene is strong enough without the setup; or (b) give Holloway a brief payoff in ch. 35 — a line establishing that she was the source of the information Webb used to find the server, or that she told Patterson about the preprint — so the setup pays off even if modestly.

### Issue 13 — Act 2: Chapter 32 Aquinas parallel better sequential than interleaved
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 32 — Modern Silence

**Problem:** The chapter intercuts between Chen and Sarah's 2025 debate and Thomas Aquinas's 1266 breakthrough in parallel columns of prose. The concept is inventive but the execution loses the thread of each voice. Following Chen's decoherence argument requires sustained attention; so does following Aquinas's theological insight. Interleaving them means neither thread can build to its natural peak before being interrupted by the other. The result is that both threads feel incomplete.

**Fix:** Present them sequentially: Chen and Sarah's full debate first, then the Aquinas scene, then Sarah reading the Summa passage and making the connection explicitly. This loses the formal parallelism but gains clarity and cumulative impact — the reader finishes Chen's argument and then sees Aquinas having arrived at the same place seven centuries earlier, which is more powerful than the two threads competing for attention simultaneously.

And now I can't pretend I don't know." Then the coda shows exactly that, without words. The verbal statement before the wordless demonstration is redundant.

### Issue 15 — Act 2: Chapter 35 Stanford motivation shrinks Webb
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 35 — The Leak

**Problem:** Webb's betrayal is explained in part by his having applied to the Stanford quantum archaeology position and not gotten it, which he blamed on Sarah's "religious entanglements." This career-frustration motivation is introduced as significant context but then undercut by ch. 37's confrontation scene, where Webb explains himself as a genuine ideological actor — he believed he was protecting science. The Stanford detail makes him seem more petty than principled, which weakens the Luke 23:34 parallel that the book needs his betrayal to carry: *they don't know what they're doing* only works if he's genuinely wrong about something important, not if he's primarily nursing a professional grievance.

**Fix:** Remove the Stanford application detail from ch. 35. Let his motivation be purely ideological from the start — he stole the data because he believed it was the right thing to do. The ideological wrong-headedness is more interesting and more thematically resonant than the career resentment.

### Issue 16 — Act 3: Chapter 39 tree-voice transition is rough
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 39 — The Reading, section "The Tree Remembers"

**Problem:** The reading scene's tree-voice section uses italicized fragmented lines (*Iron entered my grain and I split.*) — a stylistic choice used nowhere else in the book. The tree's voice throughout Act 1 and Act 2 is full sentences, careful and sustained. The fragmentary italics in this section feel like a different register imported from outside the book's established style. The content is right; the form doesn't match the voice the reader has learned to trust.

**Fix:** Rewrite "The Tree Remembers" in the tree's established full-sentence voice, without the italics. The fragmentary style was presumably meant to convey intensity, but the tree's voice conveys intensity through precision and restraint, not fragmentation. The existing Act 1 crucifixion chapters demonstrate this — the tree's most extreme experiences are described in the most controlled prose.

### Issue 17 — Act 3: Chapter 41 non-chronological placement needs framing
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 41 — Roman Welcome

**Problem:** This chapter is dated March 5, 2025 — one week *before* the reading (March 12, ch. 39). But it appears in the manuscript after the reading chapters. The dates make this parseable, but the dramatic effect is disorienting: the reader has just experienced the reading's full emotional weight, and now returns to the commission review that authorized it. There is no signal that this is retrospective or that the non-chronological placement is intentional.

**Fix:** Add a brief interior moment at the chapter's open — a single sentence in which Sarah's attention moves from the present back to that earlier meeting: something like "She had thought about Antonelli's face a hundred times since." This frames the chapter as recalled context, making the non-chronology feel like deliberate retrospection rather than an editorial error.

### Issue 18 — Act 3: Chapter 43 Tanaka scene buried
**Priority:** Medium
**Chapter:** 43 — Peer Review

**Problem:** The Tokyo call with Tanaka — one of the book's best scenes — is buried in the middle of this chapter, which covers eleven months of material. Tanaka saying "It was warm. I noted it in my lab log as a temperature anomaly. Ambient conditions could not account for it. I have not told anyone this." is a quietly devastating moment that deserves to land with full weight. Currently it competes with the preprint drama, the peer review process, and the publication beat for the reader's attention.

**Fix:** Consider splitting ch. 43 into two chapters: one covering the preprint response and Tanaka call (the immediate aftermath of the reading), and one covering the eleven-week peer review and publication. This gives the Tanaka scene the space it earns and separates the "discovery confirmed" beat from the "discovery published" beat, which are dramatically distinct events that currently blur together.

### Issue 19 — Act 3: Bertoli's title creates credibility strain in chapter 45
**Priority:** Low
**Chapter:** 45 — Still Here, "The Institute" section

**Problem:** The Institute section describes Cardinal Bertoli visiting Sarah's office "on Friday afternoons, usually without an appointment" for a year after the Institute is established. Bertoli has been described throughout the book as Cardinal Secretary of State — one of the most senior positions in the Vatican, with a schedule that would not allow casual unannounced Friday afternoon visits to a research institute for a year. The detail is warm and human but credibility-straining for the role.

**Fix:** Either clarify that Bertoli is no longer Secretary of State by the time the Institute is established (a plausible transition — he could have moved to a different role after the publication controversy), or soften "usually without an appointment" to something that acknowledges the formality of his position while preserving the warmth: "He came most Fridays, with the particular efficiency of a man who schedules what he refuses to miss."
