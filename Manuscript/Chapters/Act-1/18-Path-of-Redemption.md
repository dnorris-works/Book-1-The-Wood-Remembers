# Chapter 18: Path of Redemption

## The Ground

Good Friday, Late Morning - Golgotha

They laid the cross flat.

After everything — the arsenal, the courtyard, his shoulders, the road, the fall, Simon's hands — they laid me flat on the limestone of the hill and the stone was cold against my grain and the sky above was pale and wide and very still.

He was still standing. A soldier spoke to him. He did not move immediately, and the soldier spoke again, and then he lowered himself to the ground, lying back along my length, his shoulders finding the place where the crossbeam met the upright. His weight settled into the wood. The back of his head against the grain I had grown over a hundred years on the hillside. His arms along the crossbeam, wrists positioned.

The crowd noise dropped away. Not silence — there were still sounds, soldiers' voices, the crowd at the edges of the hill, the wind — but something in my awareness narrowed to what was directly present. Him against my wood. The sun on both of us. The smell of the limestone and the dry scrub at the edges of the hill and the iron smell of the spikes in the leather bag.

I had never held a person flat before. Not like this. When Miriam climbed into my branches she was above me, her weight distributed across several limbs. This was different — his full length against mine, every inch of his back in contact with my surface, the particular warm pressure of a living body lying down.

He was breathing slowly.

## The Nailing

The soldier with the leather bag knelt at the crossbeam.

I will not describe what came next in detail. Not because I cannot remember — I remember everything, which is both the gift and the cost of what I am — but because the details are not the point of it, and I have spent two thousand years understanding the difference between what happened and what it meant, and I am still not certain I have that right.

What I can tell you is what I felt through the wood.

The impact of the first spike transmitted through the crossbeam the way sound travels through heartwood — immediate, total, arriving everywhere at once. Not just at the point of contact but through the entire structure, up the upright, along the grain, to the far ends of the crossbeam. The cross is one piece of wood for these purposes. What happens at any point happens to all of it.

I felt him receive it.

He did not pull away. His body absorbed the impact and the specific new weight of what had been done and remained still. His breathing did not stop. It changed — a breath I had no prior name for, the breath of a person who is taking something in and holding it rather than expelling it, the breath of pure endurance at its outermost edge.

The second. Then the third — the feet, both together, the longer spike, the deeper impact.

Then it was done and the soldiers stood and the hill was quiet except for the wind and the crowd below and his breathing against my wood, which I could still feel everywhere.

## Before the Raising

We lay there together for a moment that felt longer than it was.

The sun was warm on my upper surface. The stone was cold against my back. He was between those two temperatures, his body heat bleeding into my grain from above, the cold of the stone coming up through my grain from below, and I held both of them and held him and he breathed and I waited.

I thought about the hillside. About the first winter, when I was newly rooted and the cold came and I did not know yet whether I would survive it. About the year the drought nearly took me. About the night the lightning split the tree thirty feet to my left and I felt the charge through the soil for weeks afterward. About Miriam, the weight of a child who trusted a tree the way children trust things they cannot explain.

About the man on the hillside, years before this, whose hand on my bark I had felt as something I could not account for. About that contact and how it had changed something in me that I have never found the word for.

He was that man.

I had known it since the courtyard. I know it now. The two moments — the hillside, the hill — are the same moment in the way that only very large things can contain their own beginning and ending simultaneously.

The ropes were being knotted above me. I could hear the soldiers moving into position, the shouted coordination beginning.

The pulling would start soon. After horizontal for so long, the thought of vertical again — upright, rooted in stone, the sky above — felt like something I had been waiting for longer than this single morning.

I was ready.

He breathed.

The ropes went taut.
