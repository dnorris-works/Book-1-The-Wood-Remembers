## From The Judas Rope — Chapter One

Dr. Sarah Davidson read the Vatican's announcement on her laptop in the secondary lab at half past seven on a Tuesday morning, with Chen running the calibration cycle on the tertiary scanner three feet away and the city outside the window doing what the city always did in January, which was to make the cold look architectural.

The announcement was three pages. She read all three.

The Pontifical Institute for Quantum Archaeology was pleased to announce the launch of the Relic Authentication Program. The program would apply emerging quantum methodologies to resolve centuries of uncertainty about the Church's most significant physical holdings. Objects in Vatican collections and affiliated churches worldwide would be systematically assessed for authenticity and provenance. MEMORIA would lead the scientific component.

She set the laptop down on the bench.

She had known this announcement was coming. Bertoli had warned her three months ago that the proposal was circulating internally. She had told him MEMORIA was a scientific instrument with specific design parameters, not a theological authentication service. He had looked at her with the expression of a man who had already decided what he was going to do and was giving her time to reach the same conclusion — and he had said he would manage the conversation.

She picked up her phone and called Bertoli.

He answered on the second ring.

*"You've read it,"* he said.

*"You said you would manage the conversation."*

*"I did manage it."* A pause. *"This is what managed looks like. The alternative was a full CDF inquiry into the Institute's operations, which would have produced outcomes significantly less favorable than a structured authentication program with the Institute in the lead."*

She walked to the window. Outside the January light was flat and white, making the city look patient.

Then she opened her inbox. She opened the object list Ferrante's office had already sent. Forty-three items. Bone fragments. Cloth. A nail from a church in Burgundy of uncertain provenance. A piece of braided rope.

Her eyes stopped on the rope. Three lines of description: fibrous organic material, braided structure, estimated first century. Collection of the Pontifical Institute. Source: excavation at Akeldama, Jerusalem, 2019.

She had catalogued that rope herself.

She closed the file.
